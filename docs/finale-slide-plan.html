Key insights (three, one line each)

  • 1. Good CGPA is not employability. A student can have strong grades and weak aptitude, coding or interview scores.
  • 2. Some students fall silently. The "silent decliners" segment (27 students) is invisible in grades alone.
  • 3. A prediction is useless without an action. Every recommendation needs a reason, an owner and a priority.

Business value

  • Fewer backlogs and better placement readiness by acting before results.
  • Student-facing rules are explicit: eligibility needs CGPA at least 7, no active backlog, attendance at least 75%.

The placement officer needs to know who is ready and who needs support. And the student needs a clear plan. Three insights drive the design: good marks do not mean employability, some students decline silently, and a prediction only matters if someone owns the action."

One product that predicts, explains, recommends and lets you test changes.

On the slide: the loop

PREDICT (4 outcomes, risk level) → EXPLAIN (per-student drivers) → RECOMMEND (action + owner + priority) → WHAT-IF (change an input, re-score) → back to the mentor's decision.

  • Strict mock-interview scoring: checks relevance before scoring; irrelevant, copied, gibberish or "I don't know" answers get 0; keyword stuffing is penalised; scored on the server.

Visual

A circular loop diagram with the four steps, plus a small screenshot strip: prediction with drivers, recommendation list, what-if sliders.

Speaker script (about 160 words)

"GrowthTrack is a loop. First we predict four outcomes with a risk level. Then we explain why, in plain terms, per student. Then we recommend: every recommendation names the action, the reason, the owner and the priority, and we generate over a thousand of them, so mentors are not given generic advice. Finally, the what-if simulator lets a student or mentor change attendance, LMS activity, assignments, or practice scores, and see the risk re-calculated instantly. On top we have an analytics copilot, and a mock-interview scorer that refuses to reward irrelevant answers."

Slide 4: Approach & Architecture

1:15 · Member B
A leakage-safe model pipeline feeding role-based dashboards on a secured database.

On the slide: left-to-right diagram

Data (800 students, 5 departments, 15 tables) → Features (only data up to end of semester N) → Models (logistic regression; gradient boosting only if it wins by 0.01 AUC) → Calibrated risk + drivers → Recommendation rules + priority → Role dashboards, copilot, what-if

Model pipeline

  • Python with scikit-learn.
  • Time-based feature cut before imputing, to avoid leakage.
  • Recall tuned to 0.85 in cross-validation.
  • Two-tier risk: Medium at the recall threshold, High at a stricter level, so mentors are not flooded.
  • Calibrated probabilities; train/test split of 640 / 160.
  • Same logistic models rescored in TypeScript, matching Python.

Platform

  • Next.js 16, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind v4, recharts.
  • Supabase Postgres + Auth; 45 tables.
  • Row-level security on every table; roles: admin, faculty, HOD, TPO, student.
  • Mentor sees mentees, HOD sees a department, TPO sees all.
  • Anonymous faculty evaluations; students cannot edit marks or predictions.

Student portal and admin features (the product around the models)

  • Student: Success Score home with top actions, attendance planner, results and backlog tracking, engagement points (approved activities only), aptitude test, coding test with in-browser editor, soft skills, mock interview with voice and camera preview, feedback page.
  • Admin: attendance sessions, result declaration, interventions, student directory with risk, skill gap, predictions, risk trends, segments, model page.

Visual

The six-box flow across the top; below it two small panels, "Model side" and "Platform side", listing three items each. Use one accent colour for the data-to-risk part and another for the platform part.

Speaker script (about 160 words)

"Our data is 800 synthetic students across five departments. We build features using only information available at the end of each semester, and we do the cleaning after that time cut, so there is no leakage. We prefer logistic regression for explainability and use boosting only when it clearly wins. Risk comes out as calibrated probabilities with two tiers. The platform runs on Supabase with row-level security on every table, so a mentor only sees their mentees, a head of department sees their department, and the placement officer sees everyone. The student portal and admin screens sit on top."
Source: ml/README.md, supabase/migrations, lib/access.ts, student-portal/, app/admin.

Three supporting facts

  • Risk spread at forecast: 19.5% High, 32% Medium, 48% Low.
  • Does not just copy Round 1: removing the Round 1 score changes AUC by less than 0.01.
  • Verified: 79 security checks, 21 dataset checks, and model outputs that come out byte-identical when rerun.

Why each design choice works

ChoiceReason
Cut by time before cleaningImputing first leaks future information
Recall target 0.85Missing an at-risk student is the costly error; keeps held-out recall at or above 0.80
Two risk tiersThe recall threshold alone flags about a third of students
Drivers filtered for directionRaw contributions looked contradictory because features are correlated
Logistic regression preferredMentors can read it; boosting must clearly win
Say these limits yourselves: the data is synthetic; engagement is the weakest model (about half of students flagged at recall 0.8); the backlog gain is in precision, not recall; the faculty rating is an inflated driver caused by the synthetic data.

Visual

The results table with the model column highlighted, and a small bar of the 19.5 / 32 / 48 risk spread in red, amber, green. Put a "Limits" ribbon at the bottom in a calm grey.

Speaker script (about 130 words)

"On 160 held-out students, our models beat the Round 1 score on all four outcomes. The biggest change is engagement drop, from 0.44 to 0.68. We tuned for recall because missing an at-risk student is the costly mistake. We also checked that the result does not just copy Round 1: removing that score barely changes anything. We want to be honest: the data is synthetic and engagement is still our weakest model, so we label it lower confidence."

Q&A defence sheet (3 minutes)

Answers use only what the project contains. Keep each to about 20 seconds; the team member who knows the topic answers.

Likely questionAnswer
Is the data real?No, it is synthetic: 800 students from nine archetypes, checked by 21 automated tests. The pipeline is built so real data can replace it.
Why is engagement only 0.68?It is a noisy outcome. We still beat the old score (0.44) but flag it as lower confidence, and about half of students are flagged at recall 0.8.
How do you prevent leakage?Features use only data up to the end of semester N; the time filter is applied before imputation; the dataset's diagnostic archetype column is never a feature.
Why logistic regression?It is readable for mentors. Gradient boosting is used only if it beats it by 0.01 AUC; only engagement picks it, and we note that its drivers explain the logistic model.
How are recommendations made?A rule table maps drivers to actions, with owner and priority scoring. They are tied to the student's own drivers, not generic.
What if the prediction is wrong?It is decision support. Drivers are visible and a human mentor owns every action. Students cannot edit predictions.
How is data protected?Row-level security on all tables, role-scoped views, anonymous faculty evaluations, secrets kept on the server.
Is login on?The demo signs in automatically by role area. Enforcement is built and switched by a setting.
Do all pages use the database?The role dashboards and the copilot do. Some shared admin pages still read the bundled dataset; moving them is next.
How does the interview scorer avoid rewarding junk?Four stages: pre-checks (empty, gibberish, copied question, filler, self-introduction), a relevance gate, rubric scoring (keywords, concepts, clarity) with penalties for keyword stuffing and wrong claims, and an optional LLM check taking the lower score. Runs on the server.
Is an LLM required?No. The copilot and interview LLM features need an API key; the rule-based scoring and the models work without one.
What is the Success Score?The Round 1 weighted score (academic .25, attendance .20, LMS .15, placement .15, skills .10, engagement .10, faculty .05). Bands: 75 and above On Track, 50 to 74 Needs Attention, below 50 At Risk.
